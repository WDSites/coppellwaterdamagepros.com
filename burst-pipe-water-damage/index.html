A burst pipe is different from a slow drip. A pressurized supply line, whether it is copper, PEX or CPVC, can fail at a joint, a fitting or a fixture connection. The water keeps coming at line pressure until the supply is closed. Close the main valve first, then call (817) 623-9267 and we will start planning the water removal and drying.

This page covers water from a supply line that failed on its own. If the pipe split because of a hard freeze, the thaw sequence matters, and the frozen pipe water damage page covers it. If the water came from a tank, see water heater leak cleanup.

How a supply line fails inside a Coppell house

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey reports that 68.8 percent of Coppell housing units were owner-occupied in 2020 through 2024. For most households that means one person has to call a plumber and an insurer in the same afternoon, so it helps to know the order of work before the stress starts.

Supply lines fail in a handful of ways. A compression fitting works loose, a soldered joint lets go, a braided connector under a sink splits, or a pipe rubs against a stud until the wall wears through it. Many lines run through attics and between floors, so water often travels down a wall cavity or pools above a ceiling before anything shows.

Pressure is what separates a burst from a spill. A line carrying household pressure keeps delivering water for as long as the supply stays open, which is why the first few minutes matter more than they do with a leaking drain.

Safety steps in the first minutes after a pipe bursts

Close the main water valve before anything else. The City of Coppell's winter weather FAQ tells residents that when a pipe breaks they should shut off the water as soon as possible, and the city posted a video that shows how to open the water meter lid and turn off the supply. If you cannot reach the valve at the house, use that guidance and ask the city's Public Works staff to help with the shut-off.

  • After the main is closed, open one low faucet to drain the line and ease pressure.
  • If the hot line is the source, switch off the water heater so it does not run dry.
  • Photograph the damage before you move anything.
  • Lift rugs, electronics and paperwork off wet floors.
  • Keep children and pets out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging.

Water near electricity

If water is dripping through a light fixture or ceiling fan, or reaching an outlet or the panel, stay out of the room. Cut power at the breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor. Call 911 for sparks, a burning smell or any risk of shock.

How we remove the water and dry what it touched

The plumber stops the leak. Our work starts with everything the water reached. The crew confirms the supply is closed, then uses submersible pumps or extraction wands to take standing water and wet carpet. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled inside walls and above ceilings, which a visual check cannot do.

Water from a clean supply line starts as Category 1 under ANSI/IICRC S500, and it can degrade as it sits in insulation and carpet pad. Ceiling board that has sagged, wet insulation and soaked pad come out. Where water ran down a wall, a flood cut removes the wet lower section so the cavity can dry.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then run while readings are logged each day. The same standing-water methods are described on the emergency water removal page, and the drying stage has its own page on structural drying and dehumidification.

Where the plumber stops and restoration starts

The City of Coppell states that plumbing repairs require a permit and inspection, which help make sure the work is done by a qualified, licensed plumber. Texas Occupations Code section 1301.051 lets a property owner do plumbing in their own homestead, so check with the city before you try the repair yourself.

Our crew does not replace pipe. We work around the plumber so wet materials can come out and drying can begin. Ask the plumber to leave any access opening in the wall until the crew has taken moisture readings, because that opening is often the clearest view of what stayed wet.

Insurance and a burst pipe claim

Policies differ, so read yours. A pipe that fails suddenly is usually handled as sudden and accidental discharge, while damage from a leak that has run for weeks or months is often excluded as long-term seepage. A burst supply line is a plumbing loss, not a flood, because flood coverage applies to rising surface water.

Document everything. Keep photographs and video, the plumber's invoice, and the moisture readings and equipment log from our crew. If the plumber saves the failed section of pipe, keep it. Many policies expect you to take reasonable steps to limit further damage, and closing the valve and starting drying are those steps.

Mistakes to avoid after a pipe bursts

  • Waiting to see whether a ceiling stain stops growing.
  • Closing only the fixture shutoff when the break is upstream of it.
  • Painting over a stain before the cavity is dry.
  • Running a household fan and calling the room dry.
  • Throwing wet materials away before they are photographed.
  • Turning the water back on before the plumber confirms the repair.

Burst pipe questions

Is emergency burst pipe water damage cleanup available in Coppell?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays. Close the main valve if you can, then call (817) 623-9267 and describe where the water is coming from. We do not promise an arrival time, but the dispatcher will tell you what to do while a crew is arranged.

Should I shut off the water or call you first?

Shut off the water first if you can do it safely. Every minute the line runs adds to the damage, and the dispatcher can talk you through the rest once the supply is closed.

Does a burst pipe in a wall mean the wall has to come out?

Not always. Moisture readings and a thermal image show which parts of the wall are wet. Sections that are soaked through or hold wet insulation are removed, and sound sections are dried in place.

Will my insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, but terms vary and long-running leaks are often excluded. Call your insurer promptly and give them the photographs, the plumber's invoice and our drying records.

Who fixes the pipe, a plumber or your crew?

A licensed plumber fixes the pipe. Our crew removes the water, takes out wet materials and dries the structure so the plumber and the rebuild can proceed.

How long does drying take after a burst pipe?

It depends on what the water touched. Carpet and drywall dry or come out quickly, while framing and hardwood take longer. Daily moisture readings decide when the equipment can be removed.

An appliance leak is water that escapes from a machine you own instead of from a pipe in the wall. The usual sources are a dishwasher, a washing machine, a refrigerator ice maker line and the drain hose that serves any of them. The leak can be a sudden failure or a slow drip that runs for weeks.

If water is on the floor right now, call (817) 623-9267 and describe the appliance. The dispatcher will tell you what to shut off while a crew is arranged.

Where appliance leaks start and why they stay hidden

Most appliance leaks begin at a connection, not in the machine itself. A supply hose cracks at the fitting, a drain hose slips off its clamp, a gasket hardens, or a small plastic line to the ice maker splits behind the refrigerator. Each of these sits in a spot you rarely look at.

That is why the damage is often larger than the leak looks. Water runs under the appliance, follows the floor slope and soaks the toe kick, the back of a cabinet or the drywall behind a laundry closet. By the time a stain shows, the wet area is usually wider than the stain.

Your water meter can tell you whether water is moving when nothing should be running. The City of Coppell says the last two digits after the decimal on the meter work as leak indicators for continuous flow. Check them with every faucet and appliance off, wait a few minutes, and look again.

The City also says its advanced meters let customers set a leak alert through the MyH2O customer portal. That alert will not name the appliance, but it can tell you that water is running somewhere in the house.

First-hour safety steps

Start with the machine. Unplug it only if the outlet is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Close the small shutoff valve on the supply line, usually behind or beside the appliance. If you cannot find it, close the main water valve for the house.

Water and electricity do not mix

If water has reached an outlet, the breaker panel or a power strip, stay out of the room and call 911 or your electric provider first. Turn off the circuit only if the panel is on dry ground and you can reach it safely.

Move rugs and small items to a dry room, take photos before anything is moved, and keep pets and children out of the wet area. Do not run a household vacuum on standing water. Then call us so the floor and wall cavities can be checked with a meter instead of guessed at.

How we dry out after an appliance leak

The crew starts by finding every wet surface. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show water inside walls, under flooring and behind cabinet bases without cutting everything open. Standing water is removed with a wet vacuum or an extraction unit, depending on volume.

Materials that cannot dry in place come out. That can be a swollen toe kick, a section of baseboard or wet insulation behind a washer. Air movers then push dry air across the floor and wall surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room.

The crew returns to take readings and moves equipment as areas reach the dry goal. The industry reference for this work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which sets out how water is classified and how drying is measured. Appliance leaks are usually clean supply water, which helps, but only if drying starts quickly.

Insurance: sudden failure versus slow seepage

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renters policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, and it lists a broken washer hose as an example. It also says policies usually will not cover damage from gradual leaks or seepage, and that includes mold that grows from them.

That difference is why timing and records matter. Photograph the damage before repairs, keep the failed hose or part if you can, and write down when you first noticed the problem. Your policy and your adjuster decide coverage, not us.

Coppell has a large owner-occupied share, with the Census Bureau reporting 68.8 percent of housing units owner-occupied for 2020 to 2024. The rest are renters. Renters should tell the property manager at once, because the building's policy and a renter's own policy cover different things.

Hose, valve and drain habits that prevent the next leak

  • Replace rubber supply hoses with braided stainless hoses and write the install date on the hose or on the wall.
  • Close the supply valves behind the washer when you go away for several days.
  • Pull the washer and dishwasher forward once a year and look at the floor under them.
  • Set a drain pan with a drain line under a washer on an upper floor.
  • Check the ice maker line behind the refrigerator when you move the unit for cleaning.
  • Do not ignore a small puddle, because the next one is usually larger.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency appliance leak water damage cleanup available in Coppell?

Yes. Call at any hour, since a washer hose does not fail on a schedule. Call, name the appliance and the room, and you will be told what to shut off while a crew is arranged. Do not wait for morning if water is spreading.

Can I keep using the machine until the leak is fixed?

No. A leaking washer or dishwasher puts more water on the floor with each cycle. Close the supply valve, stop using the machine and have the failed part replaced before the next load.

How do I know the water is clean supply water?

A supply hose or ice maker line carries clean water, while a drain hose carries used water from the machine. Drain leaks can contain soap and food residue, which changes how the area is cleaned. Tell the dispatcher which hose failed.

Will a small leak under the dishwasher damage the floor?

It can. Water follows the floor under the cabinet base and can swell particleboard or lift flooring edges before any stain shows. Flooring, cabinets and the subfloor are checked with a meter.

Does restoration include replacing the appliance or hose?

No. A plumber or appliance technician replaces the failed part. We dry the structure, remove materials that cannot be saved and explain what is left to repair.

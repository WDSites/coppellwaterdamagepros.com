Most real losses involve more than one stage. A burst pipe starts with removal, moves to mitigation and drying, then cleanup, and ends in repair. We plan them as one job so that nothing is left waiting on a second company. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Before help arrives

What to Do Before the Crew Arrives

  • Close the main water valve if the source is a supply line.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel on dry ground.
  • Photograph the damage before anything is moved.
  • Lift valuables and paperwork off wet floors.
  • Keep children and pets out of any water that may be contaminated, and call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas concerns.

Professional work on water and mold follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 and S520 standards, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to them.

Scope limits

What Is Not Part of Restoration

Restoration repairs damage after water gets in. It does not replace the plumber who fixes the burst fitting, the roofer who fixes the leaking flashing, the HVAC technician who clears the condensate line or the specialist who waterproofs a foundation. We can tell you what we found and which trade to call, so the cause is fixed before the rebuild begins.

That honesty matters for your insurance and your wallet. A repaired ceiling under a roof that still leaks will stain again, and a dried wall behind a fitting that still drips will not stay dry.

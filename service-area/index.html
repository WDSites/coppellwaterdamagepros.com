Where we work

Coppell and Four Nearby Towns

Coppell is in the northwest corner of Dallas County, with a small area extending into Denton County, and it borders Grapevine on the west, Lewisville on the north, Carrollton on the east, and Irving and Dallas on the south. It sits next to the northeast corner of DFW Airport, and part of the airport property is inside city limits.

That position puts four towns within easy reach for the dispatch line, and each has its own page with local details, ZIP codes and common water problems.

Quick local facts

One Fact to Know About Each Town

  • Coppell sits at about 512 feet and has Denton Creek, Grapevine Creek, Cottonwood Creek and the Elm Fork nearby, so check the city's floodplain maps.
  • Carrollton lies in parts of Dallas, Denton and Collin counties, so permit and utility details can depend on which side of a county line the property is on.
  • Irving's water is treated by the City of Dallas, according to the City of Irving, which means street-level pressure problems go to the city and indoor leaks go to you.
  • Grapevine takes water from Lake Grapevine and buys some from the Trinity River Authority, and its lake can rise sharply in a wet June.
  • Lewisville is about 14 percent water by area, with the lake, the Elm Fork, Prairie Creek and Timber Creek all within or beside the city.
Water around the area

Creeks, Lakes and Storm Season Across Our Towns

The five towns share a water map. Denton Creek, Grapevine Creek, Cottonwood Creek and the Elm Fork of the Trinity River flow through or border Coppell. Grapevine Lake sits beside Grapevine, and Lewisville Lake and the Elm Fork shape much of Lewisville. When heavy rain hits the region, more than one of these places can be affected at the same time.

In June 2015, rain from Tropical Storm Bill raised Grapevine Lake and put Denton Creek into Coppell yards, and the same event led to evacuations in Lewisville. For a homeowner, the lesson is to learn your address's floodplain status early, since homeowner policies generally exclude flood and flood policies carry a 30-day waiting period, as the City of Coppell notes.

How coverage works

Houses, Apartments and Commercial Buildings

Single-family homes make up many calls, but apartments and commercial buildings need different handling. In multi-unit buildings, a failed line in one unit can reach the ceilings below and the walls beside it, so management must close a riser or a zone valve while the restoration work starts.

For commercial tenants, the lease usually spells out who authorizes the work and whose insurance responds. Check it, tell management when you call, and keep your own photos either way.

Near a city line

City limits and ZIP codes do not always agree, and some properties have a mailing address in one town and a service address in another. The dispatcher will confirm coverage from the street address, and you do not need to work it out yourself before you call.

How Much Does Mold Remediation Cost in Coppell, TX?

No two jobs price the same. These are the factors that move the number, and the line items a written scope should show before work starts.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

By the Coppell Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026-10-28

Homeowners who ask how much mold remediation costs in Coppell, TX are usually looking for a single figure. A fair answer is that the price depends on what is wet, what is affected and how much has to be rebuilt, and any figure offered before someone has seen the space is a guess.

This guide explains what actually sets the price so that you can compare scopes line by line instead of comparing totals. The service itself is described on the mold remediation page.

Why a price range online rarely fits your house

Published ranges mix tiny bathroom jobs with whole-floor projects, and they come from different regions with different labor costs. A closet with a few spots behind a leaking air conditioner line is a different job from drywall mold across several rooms.

Rather than chase a headline number, ask what the scope covers and what it leaves out.

Factor one: how much area is affected

Area is the first driver, because more affected surface means more labor, more disposal and more time under containment. Crews measure the affected square footage and mark the edges on the wall or ceiling before they quote.

Hidden growth matters too. A small visible patch can sit on top of a larger wet cavity, and a moisture meter and thermal camera help define the true edge.

Factor two: what materials are involved

Nonporous surfaces such as tile or sealed metal can be cleaned in place. Porous materials like drywall, ceiling tile, insulation and carpet are usually removed and replaced when growth is established, because cleaning the surface does not reach the inside.

Framing lumber is treated differently from drywall. Wood framing is often cleaned by mechanical methods such as sanding or wire brushing, which takes time but saves a structural rebuild.

Factor three: containment and air control

Work that disturbs mold can spread spores through a house. Plastic sheeting, negative air machines with HEPA filtration and air scrubbers keep the work area separated from the rest of the home.

More containment means more setup time and more equipment days, and that shows up on the scope. Published practice for mold work is documented in the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it.

Factor four: fixing the moisture source

Mold needs moisture, so the cause has to be found and repaired. A supply line leak, an AC condensate line overflow, a roof leak or a sweating duct all need a plumber, HVAC technician or roofer in addition to mold work.

A remediation scope does not usually include the plumbing or roof repair itself. Ask who handles it and in what order, because remediation done before the source is fixed is wasted effort.

Factor five: testing and clearance

Some projects include independent testing before work, after work or both. A clearance report from a third party can be helpful for a home sale, a lender or an insurance file, and it is a separate line from the cleanup itself.

Independent testing comes from someone who does not do the cleanup, which avoids a company grading its own work.

What a written scope should include

  • The location and measured size of each affected area
  • Which materials will be removed and which will be cleaned in place
  • Containment and air filtration methods
  • Whether pre-work or clearance testing is included
  • The moisture source and who is repairing it
  • Disposal, cleanup and what the rebuild phase covers

A scope that lists these items lets you compare two bids on the same ground. A bid with a single total and no list is hard to judge.

Insurance and cost

The Texas Department of Insurance says most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and any mold that grows on a covered item, but they generally do not cover gradual leaks or mold from a flood. Mold cleanup and testing are often excluded unless you have added coverage, so ask your agent.

Report promptly, document the damage with photos and keep receipts. Check your policy and ask your insurer before work starts.

What to do before you collect quotes

  • Photograph the affected area and write down when and where the leak happened.
  • Stop the moisture source, or confirm that a plumber, roofer or HVAC technician has done it.
  • Do not scrub, sand or paint over the growth, and keep fans from blowing across it.
  • Ask each company for a written scope, including containment, removal, cleaning and clearance.
  • Ask your insurer what is covered before work begins, since cleanup and testing are often excluded unless added.

Frequently asked questions

Can a company tell me the price over the phone?

Only a rough direction. A reliable scope comes from seeing the affected area and measuring moisture, which is why dispatch explains how price is set rather than quoting blind.

Is mold testing always required?

No. It is common for home sales, lender requirements or disputes, but many jobs proceed with visual assessment and moisture readings alone.

Do I need to move out during remediation?

It depends on the size of the area and how containment is set up. A closet job may not require it, while a larger job may. Ask what the plan is for air control and access.

Does the cost change if the mold is in a rental in Coppell, TX?

The work is the same, but responsibility may differ. Your lease usually decides whether the landlord or tenant arranges mold work, so confirm in writing before anyone orders it.

Will insurance pay for mold remediation?

It depends on the cause. Mold tied to a covered sudden water event may be covered, while gradual leaks and flood-related mold generally are not. Ask your insurer.
